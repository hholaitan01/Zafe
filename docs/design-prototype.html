This is the very first screen a person sees when they open Zafe. Its only job is to make a strong first impression and let the user either create an account ("Get Started") or sign in if they already have one ("Log in"). We deliberately use the exact crimson gradient, logo style, and button shapes of the real ALAT by Wema app so that from the first second it feels like an official Wema product, not a random side app. This matters because our whole pitch is that Zafe could be a built-in ALAT feature — the design has to sell that idea before a single word is spoken.

This is where a new user creates their account or an existing user signs back in. We only ask for the bare minimum here — an email and password, or the one-tap "Continue with Google" option — because long sign-up forms make people give up. TEAM NOTE

This is the most important screen in the entire app — the one that makes people go "wow." After the buyer submits the deal and the chat, the AI analyses everything and shows a Trust Score from 0 to 100, drawn as a big glossy dial that fills up as it calculates. A green badge means LOW risk (safe to proceed), amber means MEDIUM (be careful), red means HIGH risk (likely a scam). Below the score is a plain-English list of exactly WHY it scored that way — for example "This seller's account is only 2 days old" or "They used pressure language: 'pay now, it's the last one'." Finally there's a one-line verdict a child could understand. This is the screen the judges will photograph and remember, so it has to feel alive: animate the dial filling, make the warning flags light up. Design TWO versions — a calm green LOW-RISK one and a scary red HIGH-RISK one — so we can show both during the demo.

TEAM NOTE

This screen handles what happens when a deal goes wrong and the two people disagree. The buyer explains the problem and can upload evidence (photos, screenshots); the seller can respond with their side too. Then our AI acts as a neutral judge: it reads both sides and the evidence and recommends one of three outcomes — give the money to the seller (RELEASE), split it (PARTIAL REFUND), or return everything to the buyer (FULL REFUND) — and it explains its reasoning in plain language. This is our standout feature: normal escrow apps make a human sit and manually settle every argument, which doesn't scale. Ours does it fairly and instantly with AI, so show that AI verdict clearly and make it feel trustworthy and even-handed to both sides.

TEAM NOTE

This is the anti-fake-receipt engine, and one of the smartest parts of the app. Instead of asking the buyer to send a screenshot of their transfer (which fraudsters photoshop), we generate a fresh bank account number that belongs only to this one deal and expires in about ten minutes. The buyer sends money to it from ANY bank — they do NOT need to be a Wema customer. The app watches the account directly, so the deal only becomes "funded" when the money truly lands. A fake receipt is useless because we never look at receipts — we look at whether the cash actually arrived. The screen should update itself to the green "funds locked" state on its own, to show the payment was detected automatically. The countdown makes it feel secure and prevents old account numbers being reused.

This is the clever lock that protects BOTH sides at once. When the money goes into escrow, the buyer is given a secret code. The seller only gets paid when they enter that exact code — and the buyer only gives out the code once they actually have the item in hand and are happy with it. That means a seller cannot get paid for something they never delivered, AND a buyer cannot lie that "it never arrived" if the code they were holding was used to release the money. One simple code makes it impossible for either side to cheat the other on the handover.

This solves a sneaky trick where a buyer receives their item but then just goes silent forever, trapping the seller's money in escrow out of spite or to pressure them.
